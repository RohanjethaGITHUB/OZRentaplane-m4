Why this plan exists

The platform as built has a gap between what admins can do and what the business actually needs them to do.

Right now, every meaningful action in the platform — registering, uploading documents, booking a flight, submitting readings, paying an invoice — was designed with the customer as the sole initiator. Admin staff can only respond: reviewing what a customer submitted, approving or rejecting it, then waiting for the next customer action.

In practice, this creates friction. Customers often need to be onboarded by staff directly. Some pay in person. Some don't submit flight readings on time. In each of these cases, the current system has no path for admin to step in and handle it — forcing workarounds or leaving records in an incomplete state.

This plan closes that gap. After it is delivered, an admin can handle the entire customer journey from their side of the platform, without requiring any action from the customer.

The guiding principle

Admin is a superuser who can act on behalf of any customer — but only for operational actions. A customer's identity remains their own: their password, and their documents. Everything else, admin can do for them.

What changes for customers

Nothing changes for customers who use the platform themselves — their existing journey is untouched. They can still register, upload their own documents, book flights, pay via Stripe, and submit readings as they do today. The difference is that admin can now do all of those things for them when needed.

What we're building

Six work items in total. Described here in plain terms — the technical specifications are held separately.

1 Admin-registered customer accounts
New

Currently, the only way a customer account gets created is for the customer to register themselves via the login page. This means if a customer comes in, calls, or needs to be onboarded directly by staff, there is no admin path.

After this is built, admin staff can create a customer account directly from the admin panel — entering their name, email address, and contact details. The system will create the account automatically, generate a temporary password, and send the customer a welcome email with login instructions. When the customer logs in for the first time, they will be prompted to set their own password. From that point, their credentials are entirely their own — admin does not have access to them.

2 Document review and approval — restored
Restoration

Admin staff previously had the ability to review customer documents, mark them as approved or rejected, and send feedback. This capability was removed at some point during the project and needs to be restored.

Once restored, admin can view all uploaded documents for each customer, approve each one, or reject any document with a note explaining what needs to be corrected. When a document is rejected, the customer receives a notification and — importantly — is blocked from scheduling any checkout flight or rental booking until all their documents have been re-approved by admin. This is a hard gate, not just a notification.

The underlying data structure for this feature is still in place; this is a restoration of the interface and the approval logic, not a build from scratch.

3 Admin-scheduled checkout flights
New

Today, once a customer account is created and documents are uploaded, the customer must log into their portal and navigate the booking flow themselves to request a checkout flight. Admin cannot do this step for them.

After this is built, admin can schedule a checkout flight on behalf of any customer directly from the admin panel — selecting the aircraft, date, and time. The booking will appear in the customer's portal as if they had booked it themselves, with a small indicator noting it was created by staff. The checkout then proceeds through the normal review and confirmation workflow.

4 Admin-created rental bookings
New

For customers who have completed their checkout and are cleared to fly, admin will be able to create rental bookings on their behalf — without the customer needing to use the portal at all. This is particularly useful for customers who book by phone, walk in, or prefer a more hands-on service experience.

As with checkout bookings, these will appear in the customer's portal and proceed through the standard confirmation and post-flight workflow.

5 In-person payment recording
Fix & verify

When a customer pays in person — cash, card tap, or bank transfer — admin needs to record that payment and have the booking properly reflect its paid status. The system currently has some capability here for bank transfers, but it is not reliably closing the full booking and invoice record. This leaves bookings in a partially-paid limbo that requires manual intervention.

This work item audits the current payment recording path, fixes any gaps, and expands it to clearly cover all in-person payment methods: cash, in-person card, and bank transfer. Admin will have a clear, consolidated panel to record the payment, enter the amount received, add a note, and confirm — after which the booking is fully closed in the system without any Stripe involvement required.

6 Admin-submitted post-flight readings
New

After every flight, customers are required to submit their meter readings — fuel, oil, tacho, and any other relevant instrument readings. If a customer forgets, is delayed, or submits readings verbally or on paper, admin currently has no way to enter those readings into the system directly. This leaves the booking in an incomplete state until the customer logs in.

This work item adds the ability for admin to enter flight readings on behalf of a customer — accessible from the booking detail view in the admin panel when a booking is awaiting or overdue for readings.

Delivery plan

Five phases, each independently shippable. The order reflects both operational urgency and technical dependency — some items need to be in place before others can be built on top of them.

1
Customer account creation

Admin staff gain the ability to register new customers directly from the admin panel, including setting a temporary password and triggering a welcome email. This is the most foundational item — it is the entry point for the entire admin-managed customer journey.

Work item 1 — account creation
2
Document review restored & in-person payments fixed

Two items that can be delivered in parallel. Document approval is a restoration of an existing capability — the underlying data is intact, making it relatively quick to restore. The in-person payment fix addresses current operational pain with customers who pay manually and have bookings stuck in an incomplete state.

Work item 2 — document approval Work item 5 — in-person payments
Can be delivered in parallel with Phase 1 if resourcing allows.
3
Admin-created checkout bookings

Once customers can be created by admin and their documents can be managed by admin, the logical next step is admin being able to schedule the checkout flight directly. This is the most technically involved item in the plan, as it requires building the foundational pattern that all subsequent "admin acts on behalf of customer" features will use.

Work item 3 — checkout booking proxy
Depends on Phase 1 (customer must exist) and Phase 2 (document approval must be working).
4
Admin-created rental bookings

With the foundational proxy pattern established in Phase 3, this item reuses that same approach for standard rental bookings. Admin can create bookings for any cleared customer from the admin panel.

Work item 4 — standard booking proxy
Depends on Phase 3 — uses the same technical pattern.
5
Post-flight readings proxy

The final item — lowest operational urgency as customers generally submit their own readings. This closes the last remaining gap in the admin journey, allowing staff to enter readings on behalf of customers when a booking is awaiting or overdue.

Work item 6 — post-flight readings
Depends on Phase 3 — booking must exist before readings can be submitted.
Alongside: Verify customer reschedule & cancel

The platform has a reschedule and cancellation request feature for customers. Before delivery begins, we should verify this is working correctly for both customers and admins — including that cancellations properly release the booking slot and that the 12-hour notice window is being enforced. This is a verification exercise, not a build.
