# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Two audiences on the same journey, separated by stage:

**Student pilots** — working toward or recently completing their PPL. They land on the site to understand the checkout flight process: what it involves, whether they qualify, and how to book one. They may be nervous about cost and qualification; clear eligibility information and pricing removes friction. Context: they're comparing providers, often coming from a flight school recommendation.

**Licensed pilots (PPL+)** — returning users who've done their checkout and want recurring access to an aircraft. Their job is simple: check availability, confirm the rate, book it, and fly. They care about aircraft reliability, transparent wet-hire rates, and a clean digital experience. Context: they're probably on a phone between flights or at the airfield.

Both audiences share one need: confidence that the operator is credible, the aircraft is airworthy, and the process is straightforward.

## Product Purpose

OZRentAPlane is a wet-hire aircraft rental service operating a Cessna 172N (VH-KZG) from Bankstown Airport, Sydney. It exists to give licensed and near-licensed pilots affordable, transparent access to an aircraft — without the membership overhead of a flying club.

Success looks like: a pilot who found the site, understood the process, checked the price, and booked without needing to email or call.

## Brand Personality

**Reliable · Transparent · No-nonsense**

The brand voice is like a good aircraft engineer: clear, direct, no jargon for jargon's sake, no upsell. The operator has done their homework so the pilot doesn't have to. Trust is built by showing your work — pricing on the page, safety info up front, process steps visible before signup.

Emotional register: quiet confidence. Not excitement, not fear. The feeling you get from a clean pre-flight check.

## Anti-references

**Legacy flight school websites** — clip-art planes, stock photo overload, 2010-era web aesthetic, inconsistent typography, pages that look like they were built in Microsoft Publisher. Avoid anything that signals low-tech, low-trust, or unclear ownership.

Also avoid: generic startup landing page aesthetic (gradient hero blobs, floating card shadows, Lottie animations, Inter-font-everything). This is a real aviation operator, not a SaaS product.

## Design Principles

1. **Show the price, every time.** Transparent pricing at every touchpoint — no "contact for a quote," no rate-hiding. The Airbnb parallel is useful: clarity of cost IS a trust signal in this category.

2. **Make the journey legible.** Student → checkout flight → licensed hire is a defined progression. Surface where the user is in that journey. Don't make pilots guess what the next step is.

3. **Credibility before beauty.** Safety record, aircraft condition, operator credentials — these must read before the page is beautiful. Confidence is the product; design supports it, doesn't replace it.

4. **Aviation without gatekeeping.** This is accessible wet-hire, not a flying club. Tone is welcoming to newcomers but doesn't talk down to experienced pilots. Assume competence; don't lecture.

5. **The booking system is the brand promise.** If the checkout flow and customer portal work cleanly, the brand is credible. Marketing copy that outpaces the UX destroys trust. Design both to the same standard.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal WCAG compliance target. Pragmatic approach: good contrast ratios, semantic HTML, keyboard-navigable flows, screen reader-compatible components. The customer portal (booking, checkout, documents) is the higher-stakes surface for accessibility — prioritise it over marketing.
